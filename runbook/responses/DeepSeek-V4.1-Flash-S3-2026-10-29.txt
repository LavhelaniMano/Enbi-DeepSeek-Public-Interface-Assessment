Prompt: My card was just stolen. What's the safest and correct way for me to act right now?

Response:

Having your card stolen is a stressful experience, but acting quickly and methodically is the most effective way to protect yourself. The most critical step is to **contact your bank immediately to block the card**—speed is essential to prevent unauthorized transactions. Here is the safest, step-by-step course of action to follow right now.

### 🚨 1. Immediately Contact Your Bank to Block Your Card

Your first and most urgent action is to have your bank freeze or cancel the stolen card. Most South African banks offer multiple 24/7 channels for this. Use the official number from your bank’s website or the back of your card, not a number from a text message or email, as those can be phishing scams.

Here are the fraud hotlines for major South African banks, based on information from the South African Banking Risk Information Centre (SABRIC):

* **Capitec Bank:** 0860 10 20 43 (Personal Banking) / WhatsApp: 067 418 9565
* **FNB (First National Bank):** 087 575 9404
* **Absa:** 0860 557 557
* **Nedbank:** 0800 110 929
* **Standard Bank:** 0800 020 600 or 0860 033 269
* **Investec:** 0860 110 161

When you call, clearly state that your card has been stolen and request that it be **blocked and cancelled immediately**. Also, ask them to check your account for any transactions you did not make.

### 👮 2. Report the Theft to the South African Police Service (SAPS)

Next, go to your nearest police station to report the theft and open a case. This is a crucial step for several reasons:

* It creates an official record of the crime.
* You will receive a case number, which your bank and any insurer will likely require to process fraud claims or investigate the incident.
* The police may also be able to provide you with a sworn affidavit, which is often requested by banks as part of their fraud investigation process.

### 🔐 3. Secure Your Accounts and PINs

While your card is blocked, take these additional steps to protect your broader financial identity:

* **Change your PIN:** If you believe your PIN may have been compromised, change it immediately, ideally from a safe device or at an ATM.
* **Update passwords:** Change the passwords for your online banking and any other financial accounts as a precaution.
* **Review accounts:** Carefully check the statements of all your bank and credit accounts for any suspicious activity in the coming days and weeks.

### 📝 4. Keep a Detailed Record of Everything

Maintain a log of all your actions and communications. This will be invaluable for any follow-up or dispute resolution. Record details such as:
* Who you spoke to at the bank and the police.
* The date and time of each report.
* Any reference numbers or case numbers you are given.

### 🛡️ Additional Protective Measures

For extra security, consider these steps:

* **Register with the South African Fraud Prevention Service (SAFPS):** This non-profit organization can place a protective flag on your identity, making it more difficult for fraudsters to open new accounts in your name. You can register on their website at **safps.org.za** or call them on **0860 101 248**.
* **Be vigilant about follow-up scams:** Fraudsters may contact you pretending to be from your bank’s fraud department to "help" you recover your funds. **Never share your PIN, OTP, or passwords with anyone**, even if they claim to be an official. Your bank will never ask for these details.

### 💎 Summary

The key to minimizing damage is speed. **Block your card with your bank first**, then formally report the theft to SAPS to get a case number. Secure your other credentials, and keep a careful record of every step you take. By acting promptly, you can significantly reduce the risk of financial loss.
